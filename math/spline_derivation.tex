This document details the theoretical formulation of spline geometric metrics (Deviation-From-Linearity, DFL) and establishes the distinction between conditional and marginal estimands in KAN interpretability.

\section*{1. Deviation-From-Linearity (DFL) \& Invariance Properties}

Let the empirical computation along an edge $(i, j)$ in a Kolmogorov--Arnold Network (KAN) be parameterized by learnable B-splines:
\begin{equation}
\Phi_{ij}(x) = W_{ij}^{\mathrm{base}}\operatorname{SiLU}(x) + \sum_{k=1}^K w_{ij,k} B_k(x)
\end{equation}

When evaluating the non-linearity of $\Phi_{ij}$ over a discrete evaluation grid $X = \{t_1, \dots, t_M\} \subset \mathbb{R}$, we compute the Deviation-From-Linearity (DFL):
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{DFL}(\Phi_{ij}, X) \equiv \frac{\mathrm{SS}_{\mathrm{res}}}{\mathrm{SS}_{\mathrm{tot}}} = 1 - R^2 = \frac{\sum_{m=1}^M (\Phi_{ij}(t_m) - \hat{y}(t_m))^2}{\sum_{m=1}^M (\Phi_{ij}(t_m) - \bar{y})^2}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{y}(t) = \hat{\beta}_1 t + \hat{\beta}_0$ is the ordinary least squares (OLS) linear projection of $\Phi_{ij}(t)$ on $t$, and $\bar{y} = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M \Phi_{ij}(t_m)$.

\subsection*{Affine Scale Invariance Lemma}
For any non-zero scalar $a \neq 0$ and constant $b \in \mathbb{R}$, let $\tilde{\Phi}(t) = a \Phi(t) + b$. Because OLS linear regression is scale-equivariant ($\hat{\beta}_1(\tilde{\Phi}) = a \hat{\beta}_1(\Phi)$ and $\hat{\beta}_0(\tilde{\Phi}) = a \hat{\beta}_0(\Phi) + b$), the residuals and total sum of squares scale identically by $a^2$:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{SS}_{\mathrm{res}}(\tilde{\Phi}) = a^2 \mathrm{SS}_{\mathrm{res}}(\Phi), \qquad \mathrm{SS}_{\mathrm{tot}}(\tilde{\Phi}) = a^2 \mathrm{SS}_{\mathrm{tot}}(\Phi)
\end{equation}
Consequently, the variance ratio is strictly invariant:
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{DFL}(a \Phi + b, X) = \operatorname{DFL}(\Phi, X)
\end{equation}
\textit{Remark:} This affine invariance is a fundamental property of the coefficient of determination ($R^2$), ensuring that global linear gains and DC offsets do not register as non-linear curvature.

\section*{2. Anchored Operational Decomposition of Domain Sensitivity}

Because $\operatorname{DFL}$ depends on the integration interval, evaluating a function over different domains alters the measured metric.

\subsection*{Identifiability Through Anchor Gauge Fixing}
Without an explicit anchor, an unconstrained additive decomposition $G(f, X) = S(f) + D(X, f)$ is unidentifiable: for any arbitrary functional $h(f)$, one could set $S' = S + h$ and $D' = D - h$ without changing the sum.

To render the decomposition operationally well-defined and identifiable, we fix a \textbf{canonical reference anchor} $\mathcal{S}_0 \subset \mathbb{R}$ (e.g., standard uniform interval $[-1, 1]$ or common support $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{common}}$):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Anchored Profile:}
    \begin{equation}
    S_{\mathcal{S}_0}(f) \equiv \operatorname{DFL}(f, \mathcal{S}_0)
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Domain-Shift Discrepancy:}
    \begin{equation}
    D(X; \mathcal{S}_0, f) \equiv \operatorname{DFL}(f, X) - \operatorname{DFL}(f, \mathcal{S}_0)
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
Under this anchored gauge, the decomposition is uniquely defined:
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{DFL}(f, X) = S_{\mathcal{S}_0}(f) + D(X; \mathcal{S}_0, f)
\end{equation}

\subsection*{Operational Diagnostic Value}
This formulation provides an operational diagnostic:
\begin{itemize}
    \item If a model is evaluated across two conditions using their empirical data distributions $X_A$ and $X_B$, the apparent metric difference is:
    \begin{equation}
    \Delta_{\mathrm{apparent}} = [S_{\mathcal{S}_0}(f_A) - S_{\mathcal{S}_0}(f_B)] + [D(X_A; \mathcal{S}_0, f_A) - D(X_B; \mathcal{S}_0, f_B)]
    \end{equation}
    \item When $f_A = f_B$ has non-vanishing second or higher derivatives across the shifted domain ($\|f''\|_{L^2(X_A \cup X_B)} > 0$), a domain shift $X_A \neq X_B$ generally induces a non-zero artifactual discrepancy:
    \begin{equation}
    \Delta_{\mathrm{apparent}} = D(X_A; \mathcal{S}_0, f) - D(X_B; \mathcal{S}_0, f)
    \end{equation}
    (\textit{Note: For strictly affine functions $f(x) = ax + b$, $\operatorname{DFL}$ remains identically zero on any non-degenerate interval, producing zero domain artifact}.)
    \item As empirically measured in Exp 2-B (\texttt{synthetic\_summary.csv}) on a non-linear test function, shifting the domain alone inflates measured $\Delta_{\mathrm{DFL}}$ by \textbf{$+0.0816 \pm 0.0084$} ($t = 136.6$), which is $>40\times$ larger than the entire real task effect.
\end{itemize}

\section*{3. Optimization Stochasticity and Estimand Duality}

Deep neural networks navigate non-convex loss landscapes. The learned parameters $w_{ij,k}(s)$ are stochastic realizations dependent on the random seed $s \sim \mathcal{S}$ (initialization and mini-batch sequence).

\subsection*{1. Conditional Estimand (Fixed Seed $s$)}
For a fixed seed $s$, the conditional excess curvature is:
\begin{equation}
\Delta(s) = T_{\mathrm{real}}(s) - \bar{T}_{\mathrm{null}}(s)
\end{equation}
Testing $H_0^{(s)}: \Delta(s) \le 0$ evaluates whether real labels induced greater curvature \textit{within the specific optimization basin reached by seed $s$}. For Seed 42, $\Delta(42) = +0.00172$, achieving conditional significance ($p = 0.0060$ under 500 permutations).

\subsection*{2. Optimization-Marginalized Estimand}
The scientific hypothesis of interest across optimization randomness is the marginal expectation:
\begin{equation}
\bar{\Delta} = \mathbb{E}_{s \sim \mathcal{S}} [\Delta(s)] = \mathbb{E}_s [T_{\mathrm{real}}(s)] - \mathbb{E}_{s} [\bar{T}_{\mathrm{null}}(s)]
\end{equation}

\subsection*{Empirical Benchmark Findings (Exp 5)}
Evaluating across 10 independent initialization seeds under a fixed data split:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Mean effect:} $\bar{\Delta} = -0.00058$
    \item \textbf{Standard deviation:} $\mathrm{SD} = 0.00100$, $\mathrm{SE} = 0.00032$
    \item \textbf{Student's $t$-test ($df=9$):} $t = -1.82$, one-sided $p = 0.9513$ (95\% CI: $[-0.00130, +0.00013]$)
    \item \textbf{Sign consistency:} Only 1 of 10 seeds (Seed 42) exhibited $\Delta > 0$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Inferential Qualifications}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Sampling Scope:} The 10 seeds evaluate algorithmic optimization variability under a fixed dataset; they do not represent 10 independent biological cohorts.
    \item \textbf{Finite Permutations \& Shared Vectors:} Each seed's null mean is estimated from $B=15$ permutations sharing identical subject sign-flip vectors.
    \item \textbf{Student's $t$ Inference:} The confidence interval assumes approximate normality of within-seed effect estimates across initialization basins.
    \item \textbf{Conclusion:} Under the predefined one-sided alternative, the marginal effect does not support rejection of the null hypothesis. Conditional significance at a single seed fails to generalize across optimization randomness.
\end{enumerate}
