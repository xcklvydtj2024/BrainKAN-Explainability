\section{Research Motivation \& Core Question}
In neuroimaging graph learning, edge-specific Kolmogorov--Arnold Networks (KANs) parameterize message-passing transformations via learnable univariate B-splines $\Phi_{ij}(x)$, enabling direct visual inspection of learned functional forms. Researchers often interpret learned spline non-linearities as evidence of task-specific biological neural computations.

\textbf{The Core Mathematical Question:} \textit{Do apparent spline non-linearities reflect task-specific computational mechanisms, or are they mathematical artifacts of operational metric definitions, evaluation domains, and non-convex optimization randomness?}

This dossier presents three concrete methodological case studies demonstrating research capability in mathematical modeling, permutation hypothesis testing, and estimand-driven sensitivity analysis.

\section{Case 1: Empirical Metric Definition \& Reference Domain Sensitivity of DFL}

\subsection{Mathematical Formulation}
Let $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ denote a learned univariate transformation evaluated on a discrete probe grid $D = \{x_1, \dots, x_K\} \subset \mathbb{R}$ with $K \ge 3$ distinct points. To quantify the \textbf{empirical deviation of sampled function values from their best affine projection over a finite evaluation grid $D$}, we fit an ordinary least-squares affine model $\hat{y}(x) = a^* x + b^*$:
\begin{equation}
(a^*, b^*) = \arg\min_{a, b \in \mathbb{R}} \sum_{i=1}^K [f(x_i) - (a x_i + b)]^2
\end{equation}
The \textbf{Deviation-From-Linearity ($\operatorname{DFL}$)} metric is defined as the normalized residual sum of squares:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\operatorname{DFL}(f; D) = \frac{\min_{a, b} \sum_{i=1}^K [f(x_i) - (a x_i + b)]^2}{\sum_{i=1}^K [f(x_i) - \bar{f}_D]^2} = \frac{\mathrm{SS}_{\mathrm{res}}}{\mathrm{SS}_{\mathrm{tot}}} = 1 - R^2
}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{f}_D = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{i=1}^K f(x_i)$.

\subsection{Analytical Nuances \& Discrete Limitations}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Directionality:} $\operatorname{DFL}$ measures the fraction of sample variance unexplained by the affine model. $\operatorname{DFL} = 0$ corresponds to perfect collinearity on $D$; $\operatorname{DFL} = 1$ indicates that the linear slope is zero and all variance is non-affine.
    \item \textbf{Degeneracy Convention:} The definition requires $\mathrm{SS}_{\mathrm{tot}} > 0$. If $f$ is constant on $D$ ($f(x_i) \equiv c$), $\mathrm{SS}_{\mathrm{tot}} = 0$, yielding an indeterminate $0/0$. In our codebase, constant functions are assigned $\operatorname{DFL}(f; D) = 0$ by operational convention.
    \item \textbf{Discrete Grid Limitation:} $\operatorname{DFL}(f; D) = 0$ guarantees collinearity only at the discrete sample points $\{x_i\}_{i=1}^K$. It does not mathematically guarantee that the continuous spline is affine in the continuum between probe points.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Synthetic Counterexample \& Domain Sensitivity}
To audit whether $\operatorname{DFL}$ behaves as an intrinsic invariant of $f$ or depends on the evaluation grid, we evaluated a static ground-truth test function $f(x) = \tanh(x)$ across two reference domains (Exp 2):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Finding:} Evaluating the \textit{exact same static function} on $D_1 = [-2, 2]$ versus $D_2 = [0, 2]$ produced an apparent metric discrepancy of $\Delta \operatorname{DFL} \approx 0.0816 \pm 0.0084$ ($>40\times$ larger than typical task differences in fMRI networks).
    \item \textbf{Methodological Qualification:} $D_1 = [-2, 2]$ and $D_2 = [0, 2]$ differ not only in their center ($0$ vs. $1$), but also in their support length ($4$ vs. $2$). Therefore, this discrepancy cannot be characterized as a pure translational effect; it reflects the joint sensitivity of the metric to domain location and scale.
\end{itemize}

\section{Case 2: Permutation Null Design \& Inferential Limits}

\subsection{Null Hypothesis Formulation \& Within-Subject Exchangeability}
In our task-fMRI connectomics benchmark, each human subject underwent both a 0-Back and a 2-Back working memory block in a paired design ($N = 100$ subjects, 200 graph samples).
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Null Hypothesis ($H_0$):} Task condition labels $Y \in \{0, 1\}$ are exchangeable within each subject $i$:
    \begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}\left((G_{i,0}, G_{i,1}) \mid Y\right) = \mathcal{L}\left((G_{i,0}, G_{i,1}) \mid \pi_i(Y)\right), \quad \forall i \in \{1, \dots, N\}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Exchangeability Structure:} The permutation distribution is generated via \textbf{subject-level sign flips}: $\pi_i \in \{(0, 1), (1, 0)\}$ with equal probability $0.5$. This preserves individual anatomy ($48$ ROIs) and functional connectome topology ($240$ edges) under the null, breaking strictly the association between learned weights and task supervision.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Monte Carlo Permutation p-value}
With network-level test statistic $T = \frac{1}{|E|} \sum_{e \in E} \operatorname{DFL}_e$ ($T_{\mathrm{obs}} = 0.17895$ for the real model), evaluating $B = 500$ independent paired permutation null models $\{T_b\}_{b=1}^{500}$ (Exp 1) yields the \textbf{Monte Carlo permutation $p$-value}:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
p = \frac{1 + \#\{T_b \ge T_{\mathrm{obs}}\}}{B + 1} = \frac{1 + 2}{500 + 1} = \frac{3}{501} \approx 0.005988 \approx 0.00599
}
\end{equation}
The 500 null values yielded sample mean $\bar{T}_{\mathrm{null}} = 0.17733$ and sample standard deviation $\sigma_{\mathrm{null}} = 0.000585$, establishing a Monte Carlo standard error of $\mathrm{SE}_{\mathrm{MC}} = \frac{0.000585}{\sqrt{500}} \approx 0.000026$.

\subsection{Inferential Boundaries}
Rather than stopping at reporting ``statistical significance,'' we explicitly articulate the inferential boundaries:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The $p$-value tests label exchangeability under the specific architecture, optimization regime, and fixed benchmark cohort $\mathcal{D}$.
    \item Rejecting $H_0$ indicates that the network weights encode task-associated variation. However, due to neural network overparameterization and parameter non-identifiability, \textbf{this statistical rejection does not prove that individual learned edge splines correspond to in-vivo biological mechanisms}.
    \item This single-seed test on a fixed cohort does not replace external validation across independent clinical cohorts.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Case 3: Sensitivity Analysis \& Directional Sign Inversion under Common Support}

\subsection{The Critical Phenomenon: Directional Inversion of Findings}
The most compelling methodological finding is that the scientific conclusion \textbf{inverts in sign} depending on whether the evaluation domain accounts for empirical support overlap.

In Exp 5, across $S = 10$ independent random optimization seeds ($w_0 \sim \mathcal{S}$), we evaluated the paired excess curvature $\Delta_s = T(Y; s) - \frac{1}{15} \sum_{b=1}^{15} T(\pi_b(Y); s)$:

\textbf{Evaluation Estimand} & \textbf{Mathematical Domain Definition} & \textbf{Mean Diff ($\bar{\Delta}$)} & \textbf{Seed Consistency} & \textbf{Student's $t$ ($df=9$)} & \textbf{One-Sided $p$} \\

\subsection{Methodological Diagnosis}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Extrapolation on the Pooled Grid:} Across 48 ROIs on HCP fMRI data, the Jaccard overlap between 0-Back and 2-Back empirical distributions averages $38.52\% \pm 5.08\%$ (covering $43.47\%$ of the pooled span). On $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{pooled}}$, splines are evaluated outside their empirical training range; boundary polynomial oscillation dominates subtle task signals, producing negative expectations for 9 out of 10 initializations.
    \item \textbf{Isolation of the Empirical Overlap Region:} Restricting evaluation to the empirical overlap region $[q_1^{(0)}, q_{99}^{(0)}] \cap [q_1^{(1)}, q_{99}^{(1)}]$ filters out boundary extrapolation noise. Under this invariant domain, 9 out of 10 seeds consistently exhibit positive excess curvature.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Research Boundary Demarcation}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Optimization Stochasticity vs. Population Generalization:} The paired $t$-statistic ($t = 5.186, p = 0.0003$) evaluates \textit{initialization consistency conditional on the fixed cohort sample and fixed 15 permutation vectors}. It does not establish generalizability across biological subject populations ($\mathcal{D} \sim \mathcal{P}$).
    \item \textbf{Shared Permutation Vectors:} The 10 seeds shared the same 15 permutation flip vectors, leaving common baseline error unmodeled across seeds ($\mathrm{SE}_{\mathrm{MC}} \approx 0.00015$ by illustrative order-of-magnitude proxy).
    \item \textbf{Label-Dependent Domain Selection:} The empirical overlap region was constructed using empirical condition distributions. While holding this grid fixed provides measurement comparability across real and null models, formal finite-sample type I error rate control under label-dependent domain selection remains an open statistical problem.
\end{itemize}

\section{Evidence of Research Capability}
This benchmark illustrates core methodological competencies essential for advanced graduate research:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Metric Scrutiny over Black-Box Metrics:} Rather than treating $\operatorname{DFL} = 1 - R^2$ as an intrinsic measure of biological curvature, we formalized it as an empirical projection residual against an affine hypothesis class and demonstrated its joint location-scale sensitivity via synthetic counterexamples.
    \item \textbf{Principled Statistical Inference:} Rather than celebrating an empirical $p \approx 0.006$ as ``proof of neural mechanisms,'' we explicitly articulated the conditioning on label exchangeability, model capacity, and dataset-specific baselines.
    \item \textbf{Estimand Awareness \& Scientific Restraint:} Discovering that the sign of the effect flips (from $-0.00058$ to $+0.00090$) between the pooled domain and the empirical overlap region prevented false scientific conclusions, demonstrating that what appears to be a biological effect can be entirely dictated by the choice of integration domain.
    \item \textbf{Future Methodological Directions:} If extending this work, the primary priority is establishing formal subject-level uncertainty quantification (via hierarchical Bayesian modeling or nested subject-level resampling), rather than scaling architectural complexity.
\end{enumerate}
